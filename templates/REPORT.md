# Five searches for an edge in prediction markets

What a small, slow participant can and cannot earn on Polymarket and Kalshi. Five studies on
public data, September 2026: four decide with a rule written in advance, one is a cross-venue
measurement desk, and one of the four is still running.

The common thread: these markets price the contract they actually settle better than simple
models do, and what a liquidity provider earns is decided by queue position and speed, which a
small participant does not have. Each study could have found an edge, and where a rule
decides, it was written before the data that decides it.

## 1. Settlement first: which price does a market resolve on?

Polymarket's 15-minute Up/Down markets name a Chainlink price stream as their settlement
source. Over {ud_windows} resolved windows (report header: "{ud_report_head}"), the sign of the
move over the window on each relayed feed agrees with the settled outcome as follows:

| feed | agreement with the settled outcome |
|---|---|
| Chainlink 60-second TWAP (time-weighted average price) | {ud_agree_twap60} |
| Chainlink 30-second TWAP | {ud_agree_twap30} |
| Chainlink spot relay | {ud_agree_chainlink} |
| Binance | {ud_agree_binance} |

A fair value priced on the spot price is priced on the wrong contract. Near expiry, an average
over the last minute has far less remaining variance than the spot price, which is where the
first, spot-settled version of the model did worst. The Up/Down results below use the TWAP
settlement model.

## 2. Taking against a fair value

Strategy: buy the side the model prices above the best ask by a threshold, with marketable
limit orders checked against the book after a latency, the fee schedule of each market, and
positions held to resolution. {ud_taker_trades} trades at zero latency across the thresholds.

- All {ud_taker_cells} cells of the grid (threshold by latency) lose, t from {ud_taker_t_lo}
  to {ud_taker_t_hi}. Taker fees are {ud_fee_share_lo} to {ud_fee_share_hi} of the loss,
  depending on the cell, and every cell still loses before fees.
- The model is not useless. Its Brier score is lower than the market mid's at 10 minutes
  ({ud_brier_model_600} against {ud_brier_market_600}) and at 14 minutes
  ({ud_brier_model_840} against {ud_brier_market_840}), but higher at 5 minutes
  ({ud_brier_model_300} against {ud_brier_market_300}).
- The gain is too small to pay for crossing the spread and the fee. In the top decile the
  model says {ud_cal_top_model}, the mid {ud_cal_top_market}, and the outcome is
  {ud_cal_top_real}: the model is overconfident where the market is cautious.

A better forecast than the mid is necessary for a taker, not sufficient.

## 3. Three strategies on resolved markets

Hourly price history of {bt_markets} resolved Polymarket markets, split by resolution date at
{bt_split}: {bt_is} markets in sample, {bt_oos} out of sample. One parameter set per family
is selected in sample by a rule fixed before the run, then run once out of sample.

- **Favorite carry** (the favorite-longshot bias says favorites are underpriced). Out of
  sample, {fc_n} trades lose {fc_loss} per trade (t = {fc_t}), and still lose {fc_loss_0}
  (t = {fc_t_0}) with no spread at all. At the mid, favorites priced near {fc_mid_95} win
  {fc_win_95} of the time, and those near {fc_mid_98} win {fc_win_98}. On this universe
  near-certainties are slightly dear, the opposite of the classic bias.
- **Dutch books** (outcome prices summing away from one). {db_no_several} of the books priced
  above one had several winners: they were nested thresholds, not exclusive outcomes, and the
  "arbitrage" was the structure of the markets. Among books priced below one, {db_yes_one}
  were valid; they made {db_yes_pnl} USD over {db_yes_n} books at one share per leg, which
  says nothing about size.
- **Hedged crypto binaries.** The best of {bh_cells} in-sample cells (t = {bh_is_t}) loses

## 4. Providing liquidity: what the resting orders earn

The first maker replay lost in all {ud_v02_cells} pre-registered cells, but its quotes stood
still between trades and it ignored the Down token, which carries most of the flow hitting the
bid. It measured its own design. The second study measures the tape instead: every print is a
fill for someone's resting order, so the realized spread of all prints says what resting
liquidity earns, with no queue model to get wrong ({ps_prints} prints, {ps_windows} windows,
{ps_days} days, split into an exploration and a confirmation sample).

- Before the rebate, the 30-second realized spread is {ps_rs30_x} cents per share
  (t = {ps_rs30_t_x}) in the exploration sample and {ps_rs30_c} (t = {ps_rs30_t_c}) in the
  confirmation sample: nothing distinguishable from zero.
- Of {ps_buckets} pre-registered buckets, one passes on both samples: prices 0.1 to 0.3 away
  from 0.5, where the liquidity earns {pq_all_stat_x} and {pq_all_stat_c} cents per share with
  the estimated rebate, of which the rebate is {pq_rebate_x} and {pq_rebate_c}.
- Queue position decides who keeps it. Fills that leave orders at their price level earn
  {pq_front_rs_x} and {pq_front_rs_c} cents before the rebate; fills that empty the level,
  the only ones that reach the last order in the queue, lose {pq_back_loss_x} and
  {pq_back_loss_c}. With the rebate they come to {pq_back_stat_x} and {pq_back_stat_c} cents
  (t = {pq_back_stat_t_x} and {pq_back_stat_t_c}), so the rule written before this split was
  computed stopped the study there.

A new quote joins the back of the queue. Without speed or an early position at a new price, a
small maker has no demonstrated edge on these markets.

of the same event, not an arbitrage.

## 7. What the five studies say together

1. **Know the contract before pricing it.** The Up/Down fair value only worked once it priced
   the 60-second average, and the cross-venue "arbitrage" with the most edge was the one whose
   two legs settle on different facts. Resolution rules are a risk factor, not a footnote.
2. **These markets are hard to beat with public information and simple models.** A
   purpose-built model beats the mid only late in the window and not by enough to pay costs,
   favorites are not cheap, and in-sample winners reverse.
3. **The remaining rent is microstructure.** On liquid markets, what liquidity provision earns
   is about the rebate, and queue priority decides who keeps it. That is an infrastructure
   business (latency, queue position, inventory across venues), not a forecasting one.
4. **Method.** Every study with a decision rule stated it before the statistic that decides it,
   confirmed it on held-out data wherever the design allowed, and reported the cells that
   failed. Independent reviews of the Kalshi pre-registration found a look-ahead in the
   selection of markets before any data was downloaded; the Dutch-book validity check showed
   that {db_no_several} of the books priced above one were nested markets, not arbitrage. Most
   of the work was in the checks.

## 8. Limitations

- Short samples: two weeks of Up/Down markets, days of cross-venue data. None of this can
  tell a change of regime from noise.
- The resolved-market t-statistics treat trades as independent. Many markets resolve on the
  same event night, so the intervals are too narrow; the signs are robust, the magnitudes less.
- All fills are simulated from public data with conservative rules, and a paper order never
  moves the market. Order placement on Polymarket is geographically restricted and Kalshi
  restricts access by country: this work reads public market data only.
- The maker rebate is an estimate from the fee schedule; the tape does not carry it.
- The cross-venue figures since inception include the first weekend of paper trading, run on
  versions of the desk that were corrected afterwards.
