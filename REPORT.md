# Five searches for an edge in prediction markets

What a small, slow participant can and cannot earn on Polymarket and Kalshi. Five studies on
public data, September 2026. The Kalshi maker study was registered before any of its data was
downloaded; the resolved-market backtest and the passive-quoting study stated their decision
rules before computing the statistic each rule governs; the taker replay and the cross-venue
desk are measurements with no decision rule. The Kalshi study is still running.

| Kalshi maker premium (kalshi-maker) | Does the published maker premium survive for a slow maker at the back of the queue? | Pending: the pre-registered backtest has not written its decision yet. |

The common thread: on the markets measured here, prices reflect the contract that actually
settles better than simple models do, and what liquidity provision earns depends on queue
position and speed, which a small participant does not have. Each study could have found an
edge; none of the four finished ones found one that survives its own costs and checks.

## 1. Settlement first: which price does a market resolve on?

Polymarket's 15-minute Up/Down markets name a Chainlink price stream as their settlement
source. Over the 2,680 resolved windows of the latest daily report ("Up/Down desk report (2026-09-23 06:09 UTC, last 7 complete days)"),
the sign of the move over the window on each relayed feed agrees with the settled outcome as
follows:

| feed | agreement with the settled outcome |
|---|---|
| Chainlink 60-second TWAP (time-weighted average price) | 100.0 % |
| Chainlink 30-second TWAP | 97.4 % |
| Chainlink spot relay | 94.3 % |
| Binance | 93.9 % |

The first six days of recording (2,548 windows) gave 98.8 % for the
60-second TWAP. A fair value priced on the spot price is priced on the wrong contract: the
first, spot-settled model had a Brier score of 0.132 at 14 minutes against
0.097 for the market. The results below use the TWAP settlement model.

## 2. Taking against a fair value

Strategy: buy the side the model prices above the best ask by a threshold, with marketable
limit orders checked against the book after a latency, the fee schedule of each market, and
positions held to resolution. Each cell of the grid (threshold by latency) trades
2,501 to 5,095 times over the same windows.

- All 12 cells lose, t from -3.4 to -1.8; 9
  of them have t below -2. Taker fees are 53 % to 87 % of the loss,
  depending on the cell, and every cell still loses before fees.
- Above zero latency the replay checks the fill against the book left by the next trade,
  which leans fills toward adverse ones (updown-desk README). The zero-latency cells are free
  of this bias, and each of them loses with t below -2.
- The TWAP model is close to the market mid in accuracy, not better in a tested sense. Its
  Brier score is slightly lower at 10 minutes (0.1177 against
  0.1186) and at 14 minutes (0.0461 against
  0.0497, on the 1,723 windows still open at that checkpoint), and
  higher at 5 minutes (0.1850 against 0.1821). No test of these
  differences was run.
- In the top decile the model says 0.979, the mid 0.933, and the
  outcome is 0.954: the model is overconfident where the market is cautious.

Matching the mid's accuracy is not enough to pay for crossing the spread and the fee.

## 3. Three strategies on resolved markets

Hourly price history of 84,160 resolved Polymarket markets, split by resolution date at
2026-06-01: 54,520 markets in sample, 29,640 out of sample. One parameter set per family
is selected in sample by a rule fixed in the configuration before the run, then run once out
of sample.

- **Favorite carry.** The favorite-longshot bias (Snowberg and Wolfers 2010) suggests that
  favorites are underpriced. Out of sample, 10,825 trades lose 0.8 % per trade (t = -6.5), and still
  lose 0.5 % (t = -4.1) with no spread at all. At the mid, favorites priced near
  95.8 % win 93.2 % of the time, and those near 99.6 % win 99.4 %. On this
  universe near-certainties look slightly dear rather than cheap. The pm-backtest README adds
  the caveat: entries on hourly last-trade prints can catch thin quotes that would not have
  filled, which inflates the apparent overpricing; only order-book data can settle it.
- **Dutch books** (outcome prices summing away from one). 54 % of the books priced
  above one had several winners: they were nested thresholds, not exclusive outcomes. Among
  the 289 books priced below one, 68 % were valid, and together they made
  43 units of capital at one share per leg: a return on capital of 19 % out
  of sample (t = 2.4), on hourly prints that say nothing about the size that could have
  been filled. The books priced above one return -0.7 %, and both sides together
  5.2 % in sample and 0.0 % out of sample.
- **Hedged crypto binaries.** The best of 9 in-sample cells (t = 4.1) loses
  9 % per trade out of sample (t = -2.4). The spot hedge reduces variance
  (hedged over unhedged variance 0.61) but protects a fair value with no edge.

## 4. Providing liquidity: what the resting orders earn

The first maker replay lost in all 16 pre-registered cells, but its quotes stood
still between trades and it ignored the Down token, which carries most of the flow hitting the
bid. It measured its own design. The follow-up measures the tape instead: every print is a
fill for someone's resting order, so the realized spread of all prints (Huang and Stoll 1996)
says what resting liquidity earns, with no queue model to get wrong (1,512,913 prints,
4,956 windows, 13 days, split into an exploration and a confirmation sample).

- Before the rebate, the 30-second realized spread is -0.26 cents per share
  (t = -2.4, a significant loss) in the exploration sample and +0.05
  (t = 0.7) in the confirmation sample. Resting liquidity does not earn the spread.
- Of 12 buckets, one passes the rule on both samples: prices 0.1 to 0.3 away from
  0.5, which on the one-cent grid means prices 0.21 to 0.39 and 0.61 to 0.79. There the
  liquidity earns +0.35 and +0.35 cents per share with the estimated
  rebate, of which the rebate is 0.29 and 0.29.
- Queue position decides who keeps it. Fills that leave orders at their price level earn
  +0.36 and +0.25 cents before the rebate. Fills that empty the level, the
  only ones that complete the last order in the queue, lose 0.56 and
  0.35; with the rebate they come to -0.26 and -0.06 cents
  (t = -1.4 and -0.4). A second rule, written after the tape
  results and before this split was computed, required these fills to pass, so the study
  stopped there. Larger orders reach deeper into the queue and carry more information
  (Glosten 1994).

A new quote joins the back of the queue. Without speed or an early position at a new price, a
small maker has no demonstrated edge on these markets. What an order at the front of the
queue, or a faster quoter, would earn was not measured.

## 5. Cross-venue arbitrage: the same event on two venues

A paper desk records both order books for pairs matched deterministically across Kalshi and
Polymarket. The figures below come from its daily report for 2026-09-22. That day: 28,769
polls, mean skew between venues 0.11 s. Of the Kalshi legs in the matched families,
88 % have no matched Polymarket leg.

- **Exact pairs** (same fact, same source, same instant; 54 on the whitelist).
  On that day, 91 detections after fees on both legs, mean edge
  0.6 cents per contract, at most 1.2 cents. The median episode is seen
  on a single poll (median lifetime 0 s, 90th percentile
  60 s); one poll later, 53 % of the intended size fills.
  Since inception, 46 pairs have resolved for a total of -93 USD,
  of which -58 USD on the 7 pairs left with a naked leg and
  -35 USD on hedged pairs; the total is not final while 14 of
  them await the second venue. In the desk's counterfactual table, 17 exact
  pairs held from their first opportunity settle with no divergence and return
  0.6 cents per contract: the loss so far is not explained by divergent settlement.
- **Basis pairs** (the same question, settled on different sources or at different instants;
  15 on the current whitelist). Held without capital from the first opportunity
  on each of the 22 basis pairs in the desk's counterfactual table, the mean entry edge is
  1.9 cents per contract against 0.6 cents for exact pairs, and the mean result
  is -7.2 cents. 2 of those pairs settled differently on the two venues, and
  such a pair pays zero or two, not one. The entry edge is the premium for that risk; on this
  small sample it did not cover it. In the first days of paper trading basis pairs were also
  traded: 8 resolved, total -302 USD, not final while
  6 of them await the second venue.

A basis pair is short the difference between two measurements of the same event. It is not an
arbitrage.

## 6. The Kalshi maker premium

Three studies of Kalshi trades find that takers overpay for YES and for longshots (Becker
2026; Burgi, Deng and Whelan 2025; Bartlett and O'Hara 2026), and the two built on tens of
millions of trades find that makers earn a positive return at settlement on average. An average over all makers is not a
strategy. This study asks what is left for a maker who improves the price by one tick, or who
joins at the back of the queue, reacts in tens of seconds and pays Kalshi's maker fees, on
non-sports markets and on trades none of those studies used.

The pre-registration fixes the sample, the statistics, the cells, the decision rule, the
holdout and the forward paper test. It was written before any trade of the sample was
downloaded and amended three times, each time before the download, after a synthetic run and
two independent reviews of the code, which found a look-ahead in the selection of markets and
a split between periods that depended on the outcome. Its SHA-256 begins with `02e4f9180ac2b8b0`. The backtest had not
written its decision when this report's sources were taken; this section is regenerated from
it once it exists.

## 7. What the five studies say together

1. **Know the contract before pricing it.** Priced on the spot price, the Up/Down model was
   clearly worse than the market at 14 minutes; priced on the 60-second average, it is close
   to the market, and in the cross-venue desk the pairs with the most entry
   edge were those whose two legs settle on different facts. Resolution rules are a risk
   factor, not a footnote.
2. **These markets are hard to beat with public information and simple models.** A
   purpose-built model roughly matches the mid and does not pay its costs, favorites are not
   cheap, and the in-sample edges went flat or reversed out of sample, except a Dutch-book
   residual too thin to size.
3. **What is left looks like microstructure.** In the one market family measured, resting
   liquidity earns about the rebate and queue position decides who keeps it. That fits the
   queue-rationing account of large-tick markets (Yao and Ye 2018), but the data here is
   consistent with it rather than a test of it.
4. **Method.** Where a study has a decision rule, the rule was stated before the statistic it
   governs was computed, the backtest, the passive study and the Kalshi study each test on a
   second sample, and the cells that failed are reported. Independent reviews of the Kalshi
   study's code found a look-ahead in the selection of markets before any data was
   downloaded, and the Dutch-book validity check showed that 54 % of the books
   priced above one were nested markets, not arbitrage.

## 8. Limitations

- Short samples: two weeks of Up/Down markets, days of cross-venue data. None of this can
  tell a change of regime from noise.
- The resolved-market intervals treat trades as independent. Many markets resolve on the
  same event night, so the intervals are too narrow.
- The rules of the passive study sit in script docstrings, and the backtest's selection rule
  in its configuration, committed with their results; nothing in the repository history dates
  them. Only the Kalshi pre-registration was committed before its data was downloaded.
- All fills are simulated from public data with conservative rules, and a paper order never
  moves the market. Order placement on Polymarket is geographically restricted, and Kalshi
  restricts access by country: this work reads public market data only.
- The maker rebate is an estimate from the fee schedule; the tape does not carry it.
